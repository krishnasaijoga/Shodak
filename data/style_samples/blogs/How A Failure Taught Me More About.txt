How A Failure Taught Me More About Myself Than Any Success Ever Could

I gave three years of my life to a dream and failed miserably.
The pain, the confusion, and the loneliness — all hit me at once.
10 years ago, I set my sights on top Engineering colleges in India. I gave it everything. And I flunked.

I was fixated on doing my undergrad at only the best like IIT (Indian Institute of Technology) or a NIT (National Institute of Technology). Naturally, anything less didn’t even cross my mind.

So following my stubborn instincts, I took a year break after 12th grade to solely prepare for it.

This dedicated year, I gave my best efforts: staying up late in coaching centers, completing workbook after workbook, never deviating from books, and giving timely mocks.

After all this, I flunked it. It took me three months to analyze my failure. It was the lowest phase in my life. I got up late, numbed out with TV, avoided my friends, and slept at odd hours of the day. I felt like a couch potato.

Failure’s Teachings

Stay Humble
I thought I was invincible until I hit the ground. I neglected both easy and tough topics, assuming I’d ace it. That arrogance left me no space to score.
No Shortcuts
I thought I could stick to what I was good at and win it all. But, this mindset is a trap — it limits your growth and leads to failure, both in exam and life.
You have to push yourself out of your comfort zone to reach any goal.
Smart Work Beats Hard Work
I studied 15 hours of a day — and still failed.
Worse — I’d look at the answers and reverse engineer fake solutions. Looking back, I can’t help but laugh 😂 — how clueless I was a decade ago? I truly thought I was clever.
Have a Strategy
I had no goals, no timeline, no to-do lists, no plan. I didn’t even glance the whole syllabus.
How can I win a battle not knowing what I was fighting?
Be Curious, Not Just Competitive
I wasn’t learning — I was memorizing. My curiosity was dead. This showed up every time I faced a question that didn’t follow the script.
Control What You Can
I worried a lot on questions like:
“Whether I can crack it?”
“What do I have to say if I don’t succeed?”
“Would my friends laugh out loud on my face for taking a year break?”
None of that helped. What did matter? Knowing the syllabus, planning well, and believing in myself without ego.
Master The Basics, Not Just The Answers
I treated mathematics like formulas and science like rules.
Never asked “Why?”
This blind obedience is when I started digging my own grave.
Real understanding begins with curiosity.
Looking back, I don’t regret those silly mistakes. I am grateful for them, they made me who I am today.

Those lessons taught me humility. They made me reflect on myself. They showed me the power of strategy, the value of smart work, and the fun of learning. Most of all, they taught me about myself and help me rise again.

This failure didn’t define me — it redefined me.
And honestly, I wouldn’t trade it for anything.
