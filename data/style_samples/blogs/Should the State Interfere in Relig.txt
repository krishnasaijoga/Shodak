Should the State Interfere in Religion?

On April 2, 2025, the Indian Lok Sabha passed the Waqf Amendment Bill, also known as UMEED Bill (Unified Management Empowerment Efficiency and Development). Due to its implications for Muslim religious freedom, the bill has sparked a debate on state interference in religion.

State and Religion

A secular state is expected to maintain a separation between state and religion. However, in India, religion plays a crucial role in the economy, politics, and social & cultural domains. Thus, the definition of secularism binds the state to regulate religious affairs.

Secularism varies across different regions of the world. For example, France enforces complete separation between state and religion, whereas Vatican City is ruled by the Pope, the head of the Catholic Church.

The global debate centers on whether the state should completely separate from religion, as in France, or interfere situationally, like India.

Why does State Interfere in Religion?

Protect Secularism: Enacting laws that uphold secular ideals. Ex: Ukraine’s ban on the Russian Orthodox Church for spreading pro-Russian propaganda during wartime.
Protect Rights of Citizens: The abolition of untouchability in India through Article 17 of the Constitution.
Transparency: Bring financial, transactional, and managerial transparency in the management of large religious institutions. Ex: Waqf Amendment Bill in India.
Protect Public Order: Prevent atrocities, such as the Holocaust during World War II.
Prevent Superstitions: Eliminate harmful religious practices in the modern world. Ex: State of Maharashtra's Anti-Superstition and Black Magic Act, 2013 to prevent practices like human sacrifice.
Prevent Political Misuse: In a democracy, the state must safeguard voters’ rights by preventing the exploitation of religious sentiments and resources in elections.
Support Broader Development Goals: When infrastructure projects conflict with religious sentiments, government mediation can help find a fair solution.
Protect Culture: Minority religions may lack resources to safeguard religious structures, artifacts, and literature. In such cases, state intervention can help preserve them. Ex: Japan’s 1897 law to preserve ancient temples and shrines
State interference in religion is not arbitrary; it aims to ensure political, cultural, and economic stability.

Let us learn why this interference is criticized.

Why is State Interference Criticized?

Opposes Religious Freedom: Countries like India explicitly provide religious freedom through Articles 25–28 of the Indian Constitution, but interference opposes this principle.
Loss of Autonomy of Religious Institutions: State interference can erode the religious institutions’ powers and traditions. Ex: Removal of the Dalai Lama’s influence from Tibet by the Chinese government
Interference from Non-Religious Entities: Bureaucratic control over finances of religious institutions. Ex: Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments Act (HR&CE) granted state government departments the power to oversee Hindu temples
Risk of Alienation: Soviet Union’s state atheism and suppression of religion led many to abandon faiths.
State Competency: The state may not always be the best authority to determine the appropriateness of religious practices, rituals or traditions. Many traditions hold deep significance for believers, even if they seem outdated in the modern world. Ex: The Indian Supreme Court’s 2019 overturning of the Kerala State High Court’s ruling on women’s entry into the Sabarimala temple.
Political Manipulation: Misuse of religious places for political gain. Ex: Pakistan’s exploitation of mosques for political extremism
Excessive interference violates fundamental rights, personal beliefs, and religious sentiments; therefore, it cannot be unlimited or arbitrary.

Middle Ground: Balanced Approach

Based on the above arguments, we can conclude that the state should not interfere in faith, religious practices, or beliefs unless they pose harm to society. Moreover, any interference cannot be without involving affected religious communities. Interference can also be used to ensure equality among all religions and encourage dialogue between religious institutions to resolve issues.

Conclusion

A state must ensure that its interference in religion is limited, necessary, and impartial. The objective should be to uphold religious freedom while protecting basic human rights, public order, and societal progress — while engaging religious communities in the process. Striking this balance is crucial for fostering religious harmony in a diverse society.
