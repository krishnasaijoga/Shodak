Things I Wish I Knew Before Starting UPSC Preparation


Before you dive into your UPSC Civil Services Examination (CSE), let me hand you a life-jacket I wish I had. Please give yourself a pat for taking this brave path — it’s not an easy decision to take.

Before we get into it, let me introduce myself. I am a proud failed UPSC aspirant — 4 attempts, zero qualifications and no regrets. Why? Because I chose a path of purpose over passivity.

It’s up to you — take it or leave it — here’s what I wish someone had told me before I started.

Lessons from Experience

PYQ Analysis
Don’t just solve — dissect them. We hear toppers saying that PYQ analysis is their key to success, but they rarely explain how.
Merely knowing the answer isn’t enough— find keywords, replace them with alternatives, connect to current affairs, tweak options, and find the theme of each question.
Do this until you internalize UPSC’s thinking pattern.
Limit resources, Expand Understanding
Toppers and mentors often advise to limit resources and they are right. Reading the same content from 5 books only fragments it. Always aim for cost-benefit efficiency — fewer resources and deeper grasp.
Do Not Buy Books in Bulk
Rookie mistake — hoarding materials.
The mountain of materials only pressures you. Instead, pick one subject, buy or borrow a basic book like NCERT, and finish it.
Set smaller milestones to conquer bigger ones.
Write Smart Notes, Not Just Short Ones
Avoid writing obvious facts like “President is elected indirectly for 5 years”. Instead write down “Article 53 vests executive power of India to President and is exercised by her directly or through officers subordinate to her.”
Avoid full sentences; use shorthand.
This boosts recall, and revision speed.
Diversify Your Schedule
Don’t over-focus on single subject and ignore others.
I’ve seen aspirants dive deep into economics only to lose out on environment, which carries similar weightage(~14%).
Every mark matters in competitive exams. Don’t leave gaps.
Be Mains Ready, Before Prelims
Prepping only for prelims is a trap. If you clear it and haven’t touched mains, you will have only 4 months to clear mains — including the optional subject. This is nearly impossible to manage.
Next year you start with less time for prelims and again ignore mains.
This cycle of ignoring mains can lead to loss of nearly 3–4 attempts without realizing.
Prepare smart. Prepare for both
Protect your Golden Hour
The first hour of your day sets the tone.
If you use it well, chances are you use your day productively. Don’t start with your phone or TV — dive straight into study.
This habit can save weeks of wasted prep time.
100% is a myth
Instead, aim to cover maximum possible syllabus and start giving early mocks. That’s how you learn the pattern, traps, and the mindset of UPSC.
Make space for self
UPSC preparation isn’t a straight line but a Sine curve.
So schedule short breaks, ideally half a day or less. These intentional breaks save you from burnout.
The Brutal Reality of competition
The clearance rate is less than 0.1%, which means UPSC only selects candidates to fill vacancies — there is no consolation. If you don’t clear it by a mark, your entire year restarts.
Even if you do a million things correctly, there is a chance you might end up on the wrong side of the line — that hurts.
It is just an exam.
It doesn’t decide who you are. What you choose to do next does.
Have a Plan B
UPSC CSE is a low success game, so be prepared.
Be pragmatic. Give yourself time-based targets to achieve, if there’s no success — try to understand that there is something bigger waiting for you.
Make a plan that has better odds like upskilling, certifications, job, and further studies.

Conclusion

After 3 years of preparation, I may not have cracked UPSC but it cracked me open into someone who is more aware, resilient, and more grounded than ever.

Like Sree Krishna from Bhagavat Gita says, “karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana | mā karmaphalaheturbhūr mā te saṅgo ‘stvakarmaṇi ||”

Focus on performing your duty without attaching it to the outcome, and don’t let inaction be your path.

This mindset kills fear and fuels progress.

All the best for your journey, make it memorable for yourself not for society.
