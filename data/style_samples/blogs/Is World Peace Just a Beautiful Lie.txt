Is World Peace Just a Beautiful Lie?

You wake up to a golden sunrise, sip a coffee, and read the newspaper headline: “10 Years With No War”, “Global Temperatures Down By 1°C”.

To many, it sounds like fiction — world peace is often dismissed as a myth. But not everyone agrees. Optimists see signs that world peace is not only possible but already taking shape.

Let’s first explore why many believe world peace remains just a dream.

World Peace is a Myth
The ongoing Russia-Ukraine war, Israel-Hamas war, USA reciprocal tariff, and ongoing tensions between nations fuel concerns of world war and dim hopes for world peace.

Resource Competition
Scarcity of water, minerals, food, and energy always leads to conflicts — especially when fueled by unequal distribution, poverty, and climate stress.
Persistent Human Conflict
The inherent nature of humans — driven by competition, power struggles, and historical grievances — make world peace unattainable.
From civil unrest in the Democratic Republic of Congo to border skirmishes in Kashmir and conflicts across the Middle East, history teaches us that conflict is the only constant.
Empirical Evidence
More than 150 countries are at loggerheads and a handful can be claimed fully at peace. So, it’s viewed by some as more of a utopian goal than a practical one.
Realist Theory of International Relations
Realism asserts that the international system is anarchic: states act for their self-interest for survival.
According to thinkers like Hans Morgenthau and Kenneth Waltz, peace is temporary; conflict is inevitable due to power struggles and security dilemmas.
Nationalism
Some political philosophers, like Thomas Hobbes, argue that humans are inherently driven by fear and desire for power, making conflict natural.
Nationalist ideologies have repeatedly led to wars. For example, India-Pakistan rivalry, Rwanda genocide.
Global Militarization
Global military spending hit $2.4 trillion in 2023, a number that reveals more about our global priorities than we often admit.
And yet, all hope is not lost. The world has shown us glimmers of hope through cooperation and compassion.

World Peace is Not a Myth
Moral and Practical Necessity
Peace is an essential — not just for global cooperation but for human dignity and survival.
Rise of Global Consciousness
The rise of movements like antiwar protests and victim solidarity reflect growing consciousness on peace.
For example, the mass protests in Morocco against the Israel-Hamas war.
Economic Globalization as a Peace Incentive
Trade dependencies in a globalized world play a significant role in discouraging war, as they incur huge losses.
For instance, trillions of dollars were wiped from global markets due to Trump’s reciprocal tariff announcement
Hope
According to Global Peace Index 2022 data, there is a reduction in intensity of violence worldwide. This shows that, while it’s challenging to achieve world peace, it’s possible with effective strategies.
As with many complex issues, truth may lie somewhere in between.

The Middle Ground
Peace as an Ongoing Process
Several theorists argue that peace is not mere absence of conflict but an ongoing process involving justice, equity, and respect for differences. The struggle for peace is seen as essential even if it’s an elusive goal.
Human Nature and Contradictions
Some contributors argue that desire for peace coexists with tendencies towards conflict.
Conclusion
Whether world peace is a myth or distant reality depends on the theoretical lens and how we define “peace”. If we define it as absence of all conflict, then it’s an unattainable ideal. But if it means reduction in violence and cooperation through institutions, then it’s not only possible — it’s observable in many regions like Mozambique, Liberia, Philippines, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Timor-Leste, and others.

If you believe world peace is possible, you’re not alone — you’re not entirely wrong.
