Cancel Culture Is a Mob, Not a Movement

Recently, India agreed to halt military action with Pakistan amid the ongoing escalations and operation Sindoor. This is hailed by international communities. But the decision sparked mixed reactions within India.

Some felt that India lost its upper hand due to the ceasefire. Online trolls turned their fury towards Mr. Vikram Misri, Foreign Secretary of India, the public face of the operation. He took care of leading press releases and answering public queries.

The trolling spiraled, even targeting his wife and daughter are not spared. They were harassed on social media platforms like X, Instagram, and LinkedIn.

This incessant trolling against a civil servant is not what they deserve. They deserve respect and our support — only they can work for the nation effectively.

Welcome to this digital age where opinions are currency and outrage is the new social order. And, we are all either spectators or the next ones lined up for execution.

Evolution of Cancel Culture
It evolved from the practices of social ostracism and boycott. But the phenomenon is amplified by digital platforms. Modern cancel culture is characterized by rapid, collective online responses that can lead to real world consequences for those targeted.

Moral Policing
The main premise of cancel culture is holding one accountable, which requires context. But the cancel culture doesn’t offer this. It only offers retweet, rage, and reputational ruin.

Expressing one’s opinion is censored by the mob behind keyboards. In the era of sensationalism, a ten-year-old tweet becomes a death sentence and an awkward joke becomes character assassination. When an apology is given, it is either too sorry or not sorry enough.

The internet is a courtroom where the judge, the executioner, and the witness are all anonymous and do not have any obligations to facts — only feelings. And the punishment is not rehabilitation but erasure.

This isn’t justice. It is bloodsport dressed in social progress. This mob doesn’t believe in growth, they do in making people vanish.

Who Gets Cancelled
The uncomfortable truth is that this culture isn’t democratic but chaotic. It is driven by power not principle.

Celebrities with PR agencies survive and even turn the narrative around. We have many examples of Bollywood celebrities whose image changed from anti-national to deshbhakt in a span of months.

But, what about government employees, independent writers, journalists, and unknown artists? Their lives are ruined and careers uprooted.

It is not the wicked that get canceled but the weak.

What is Really Getting Cancelled
Cancel culture kills conversation — healthy debate on topics that are neither white nor black. It fuels chilling effect, encourages censorship, and silences intellectuals.

Even after the truth is known, the impact of cancellation doesn’t end. The burden of proof remains on the victim.

We don’t educate — we cancel.
We don’t evolve — we exile.

In the name of progress, we are breeding fear in thought, speech, action, and art.

Cancelling ≠ Accountability
We all agree that people who do harm have to be called out. But this has blurred the line between intellectual thoughts and emotions.

Real accountability is hard. It requires us to engage, to listen, and to forgive. Mobs never do that, they burn.

In the end, cancel culture wouldn’t create a better world — it creates one where the good remains silent and leaves the bad shouts louder.
