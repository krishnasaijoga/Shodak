How I Built Self-Confidence And How You Can Too


Imagine leading an NGO initiative where you are tasked to organize a 3-day-outdoor camp called the Dream Camp — designed for shelter home children whom you support academically.
You make all the arrangements — venue, food, transport, sessions, props, and planning.

A day before the camp, while making the final arrangements at the venue, you get a call from the shelter authorities: the children are protesting and don’t want to attend the camp. They’ve even torn up the invitation letter.

Your heart sinks.

Two lakh rupees in donations and your NGO’s city chapter’s reputation were on the line — and the very children the camp was meant for refuse to attend.

This was my situation in 2019, when I was the Dream Camp Lead for Make A Difference (MAD) chapter in my city.

I had backup plans for technical failures — plans B and even C — but never imagined that the children themselves would rebel. These were the same kids who wait all the year for this camp.

It took me 20 minutes to gather myself.

I made few calls, talked to shelter authorities, and sent volunteers to speak with children and understand what is wrong.

Ironically, the issue stemmed from one of our own volunteers — someone who was not allowed to the camp. He badmouthed the rest of us to the kids. They demanded that the excluded volunteer be a part of the camp.

Eventually, the shelter authorities stepped in and confirmed the children would attend after all.

Even at such times, I didn’t panic or lose confidence. I believed we could turn things around — and we did it.

At the end of the 3-day camp, children gave us a heartfelt feedback. They loved every part of it — the theme, activities, bonfire, story telling, sandwich making, and music sessions.

Things spiraled out of control, but I regained it. I could do it because of self-confidence I had built as a volunteer.

How I Built my confidence And How You Can Too
1) Get Your Hands Dirty — Try New Things
Volunteering has changed me, from someone who said “I will do it” to someone who could proudly say “I did it.”

Before volunteering, I rarely did anything outside academics. Just because I didn’t want to try and fail.

I understood that I couldn’t stay the same. So, I started trying new things.
I failed at some, I succeeded in others, but most importantly — I learned from my mistakes.

Lesson one. Try new things. With every attempt — win or lose— you’ll grow more confident.

Let us breakdown why we feel less confident.
Because we fear.
And why do we fear?
Because we do not understand something.

The only way to beat that fear is to start. To try. To learn through doing.

2) Research Before Start
Before jumping into a task gather data. Knowledge builds confidence.

Understand different paths to success— know what works, what doesn’t, and what fits you.
Compare your options and choose one that aligns with your strengths and situation. Borrow techniques from other paths if required.

This gives you a roadmap — failures to avoid, the milestones to reach, and a timeline. With clear picture, you’ll feel more equipped to handle whatever comes your way.

3) Believe In Your Path
This step is more emotional than logical. Once you have chosen your path — own it.

Yes research helps. But, belief fuels action.

Commit fully. Treat your chosen path like your Bible or Quran or Gita.

When doubts creep in — yours or others’ — belief in your decision gives you the strength to push through. And when you push through, confidence naturally follows.

4) Perseverance — Enemy of Failure
Let me share another story from my volunteering days before the dream camp. This time we were organizing a volunteer induction session in our city.

Our team of eight city managers have to handle everything — venue hunting, volunteer management, campaigns, and coordinating with our city director, who comes down from head office.

Just a day before the event, our venue partner unexpectedly shifted venue to a far less suitable one. We had spent weeks securing such a good venue at minimal or no cost — so we could use the saved funds for shelter home children.

Now, without the time and resources for a new venue, I gave up. I suggested we postpone the event.

But my team didn’t even consider that option. They simply said, “We’re doing this event tomorrow, no matter what!”

Their resilience lit a fire in me.

We jumped into action — making calls, talking to venue owners, and searching the entire city. One teammate even got injured in a minor bike accident in the process. Still, we didn’t give up.

It was a roller coaster ride yet, we pulled it off.

That’s when I realized that failure wins when we give up at our lowest. If we fight back, we move closer to our goals.

Perseverance doesn’t just improve outcomes — it builds confidence.

5) Clarity of Thought
Every time I hit a roadblock, I return to my purpose.

When volunteering I believed that every small success was one more step closer toward helping a shelter home kid.

This clarity helped me push through fears and doubts.

Even on the night before the Dream Camp when kids rebelled, I believed I cannot be the person who let them down.

Cause above self gave me the clarity of thought. This boosted my confidence to take up any challenge with a smile.

6) A Good Support System
I would be lying if I say that this journey was all self-made. I’m not the sole guru of my own growth.

My first real experience of resilience came from my teammates. Their unwavering confidence boosted mine.

My family helped me too. They’ve reminded me of my potential, even when I doubted myself. Sure, they’ve been disappointed when I failed — but that comes from a good place of love and belief. They knew I could do better.

When they take an extra step to support and believe in you, it becomes easier to take an extra step to believe in yourself.

And this is how I built my confidence.
It came through challenges, countless moments where I had to rise above my own doubts.
It shattered the shackles of low-esteem.

Confidence isn’t built in comfort — it’s built in motion. So try new things. That’s how it all begins.
