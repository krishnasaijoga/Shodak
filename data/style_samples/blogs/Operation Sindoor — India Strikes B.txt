Operation Sindoor — India Strikes Back After Pahalgam Massacre

At dawn on May 7, 2025, India woke up to a different kind of alarm — precision airstrikes deep inside Pakistan and PoK, dubbed as Operation Sindoor.

The name itself — “Sindoor”, a traditional vermilion worn by Hindu women on the forehead, symbolizes the color of sacrifice and resolve — setting the tone for a high stakes counter-offensive by India.

It was in response to the two-week old Pahalgam terrorist attack on tourists in Kashmir. The attack took 26 lives and traumatized many. It was a desperate attack on civilians to send a clear message that Kashmir is not safe.

So, India decided to respond with an even clearer message, “India doesn’t look down, it knows how to secure itself and deter terrorism even on foreign soil.

Impact of Operation Sindhoor
Sindhoor is the name of the operation, involving all three services of Indian military — Army, Navy, and Air Force — focused on 9 different sites with precision. It was strategically targeted on terrorist campsites and no civilian or military sites were targeted, according to the Indian Defense Ministry.

The operation was targeted on camps of Lashkar-e-Taiba (LeT) and Jaish-e-Mohammed (JeM) in Bahawalpur, Murdike, Sialkot (in Pakistan), and Kotli, Muzafarrabad (in PoK).


Locations struck by the Indian Forces. Source: Hindustan Times
In response to this, heavy shelling was seen along the Line of Control (LoC) that separates India and Pakistan-administered Kashmir. India stated that at least eight civilians were killed due to Pakistani shelling.

International Responses
Pakistan reported 26 casualties and 45 others injured. Pakistan also claimed to have shot down six Indian aircraft (though these claims couldn’t be verified independently) and vowed retaliatory action. Pakistan termed India the “aggressor” and asserted its right to respond.

United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres called for maximum restraint, stating that the world cannot afford a military confrontation between India and Pakistan.

The USA echoed the call of restraint but re-affirmed India’s right to defend against terrorism.

China urged military restraint between the nuclear-armed nations and expressed regret over India’s action.

Geopolitical experts believe that the scale of the Indian strikes is larger than the Balakot airstrikes of 2019, which were in response to Pulwama attacks. This time, India crossed international borders and a sizeable response from Pakistan is expected.

Unanswered Questions
India’s right to defend is recognized. And its strategic exposure of terrorist camps across the border has drawn global attention.

However, key questions remain unsolved — who are the on-ground perpetrators? Where are they now? Without capturing them, the link to Pakistan remains speculative.

Questions from past attacks — source of the military grade RDX used in Pulwama attack and intelligence lapses— are still unanswered. India must not repeat this mistake. Investigation must accelerate alongside military response to ensure accountability and justice.

The Future
Grey clouds have once again loomed over South Asian region once again. The region’s stability depends on future de-escalation and decisive action from both the nations.

For a long time, Pakistan has been harboring, training, and exported terrorism from its soil. This operation once again reveals that camps of internationally sanctioned terrorist groups like JeM and LeT continue to exist.

It is time Pakistani citizens demand answers from its government and army: why their homeland is still a breeding ground for terrorism? Why a significant portion of their tax money is spent to disrupt peace even during crisis times.

If peace is to return to the region, it won’t just be through missiles but through truth, accountability, and the justice that leads the way.
