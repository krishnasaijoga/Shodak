Why Governments Prioritize Space Science Over Welfare — And Should They?

The return journey of Sunita Williams and Butch Willmore, after accidentally getting stranded over nine months on the International Space Station (ISS), was celebrated globally as a triumph of human spirit and space science. Originally intended as an eight-day mission, critical failures in Boeing Starliner spacecraft extended their stay dramatically.

While this success was appreciated, it’s important to note that nearly $125 million in loses were incurred by Boeing for this mission. This loss was borne indirectly by the US government through earlier grants and contracts.

Imagine if that money had instead funded underprivileged students or clean water initiatives for millions.

So why do governments around the world pour in billions into space science, while millions on Earth still lack food, education, or clean water?

What is Space Science and Why We Do It?

Space science is a broader field of study of astrophysics, cosmology, planetary science, and space environments. It helps us explore the origin, evolution, structure, and mechanism of the cosmos and everything in it. It also involves finding our place in this universe.

Like all sciences, its ultimate goal is to improve human life and deepen our understanding of nature. Beyond discovery, it offers technological, societal, political, and environmental benefits. It also cultivates a mindset of inquiry, rationality, and collaboration. Both its outcome (scientific knowledge) and process (scientific inquiry) enrich humanity.

Why Space Funding Is Justified?

Technological Advancements
Space science has led to innovation in weather prediction, satellites, GPS systems, surveillance systems, high-resolution cameras, clean energy technology, and medical imaging technologies.
Economic Growth
It creates high-skilled jobs in markets like engineering, aerospace, and advanced manufacturing. Projected to grow from $630 billion in 2023 to $1.8 trillion by 2035, space science promises high ROI (Return of Investment).
Counter Global Challenges
Global challenges like climate change, disaster response, and terrorism can be countered with space technology and knowledge. For example, Satellite imagery is used to measure greenhouse gas emissions, forest cover, coral health, and wetland management.
Societal
Fosters a mindset of science, rationality, inquiry, and collaboration. Countries can collaborate on improving collective understanding of our origins, which has far reaching implications.
Wonder and Inspiration
Humans are naturally drawn to exploration, curiosity, and the awe of the unknown. Space research taps into this drive and deepens our desire to understand Earth and beyond.
Long-term Survival
Space research lays the groundwork for our future — from asteroid defense, Mars colonization, and offering backup plans for civilization.
The Case Against Space Funding

Social Inequality
While millions of people live in poverty or lack basic human services, billions are spent on ventures beyond Earth — often ignoring the cries for help from within it.
Limited Direct Benefits
The direct impact on everyday life remains limited. Money can instead be spent on hospitals, schools that deliver more immediate value.
Opportunity Costs
It‘s often cheaper and impactful to train doctors and teachers than astronauts and aerospace engineers.
Environmental Harm
Rocket emissions in stratosphere lead to climate forcing. Rising space debris raises the threat of Kessler syndrome, which could jeopardize future missions.
Uncertain ROI
Despite optimistic projections, space research involves high risk, long timelines, and no guaranteed return especially in deep space missions. Past tragedies like the Columbia and Challenger shuttle remind us of the human cost.
Risk and Failure
A setback or a failure is a loss of taxpayers’ money, this delays progress and further expenditure. Technologically sustaining human life in space is costly.
Distraction from Earthly Problems
Critics argue that colonization of space diverts attention and action from Earth-bound problems like climate change, poverty, and global health crisis.
Ethical Concerns
When benefits reach only to a fraction of humanity, is such funding ethically justified?
Balanced View

While stars seem a distant dream, the ripple effects of space science are felt on Earth — easing healthcare, telecommunication, technological advancement, inspiration and education, weather prediction, disaster management, and finding solutions to global challenges.

Yet, this shouldn’t eclipse the immediate basic needs of humans.

The choice is not binary — it’s about striking a strategic balance and recognizing their complementary roles. Space research drives innovation and long-term societal benefits, while welfare addresses human immediate needs.

After all, investing in space isn’t about abandoning Earth — but ensuring we never have to.
